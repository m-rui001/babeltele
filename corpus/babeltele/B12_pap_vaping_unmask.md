Chest CT typically reveals diffuse ground glass opacities, patchy geographic patterns, and occasionally a crazy paving appearance—the latter being rare in secondary PAP. Pulmonary function tests may show reduced carbon monoxide diffusion, though this finding is not specific to PAP [ 5 ]. Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) is a key tool for diagnosing PAP, typically showing milky fluid and PAS-positive proteinaceous material with few inflammatory cells.

The presence of antibodies against GM-CSF is highly sensitive and specific (100% and 98% respectively) [ 7 ] and is a helpful non-invasive method in diagnosing PAP. PAP treatment typically includes supplementation with GM-CSF (commonly administered via inhalation), anti-CD20 therapy, plasmapheresis, and whole lung lavage (WLL). WLL is considered the standard procedure for severe cases with significant hypoxemia; it is performed on each lung separately under anesthesia and may be repeated as needed until improvement is observed.
