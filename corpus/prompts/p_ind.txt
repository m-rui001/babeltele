You are the lead forensic engineer on a refinery failure investigation. A renewable-diesel hydroprocessing unit was restarted after a 21-day turnaround. Six weeks later, during a startup at 74 % of design rate, the outlet tube of the fired heater ruptured, releasing hydrogen at 62 bar and light feed, causing a fire.

Measured facts you are given:
- Tube metallurgy: 9Cr-1Mo (P9C), design metal temperature at the outlet 510 °C; the last recorded outlet tube skin thermocouple read 596 °C, but the thermocouple was one of a pair whose second element had failed open-circuit 11 days earlier and had not been replaced.
- Decoking: the unit is steam/air decoked online. The last decoke ran 3 h longer than the previous cycle and the boiler house steam pressure fell from 24 bar to 15 bar mid-cycle. Combustion air registers for the lower burner row were found after the shutdown with the linkages hand-tight against the stops.
- Creep data for the exchanger tube, taken from a coupon: rupture life at 540 °C under 120 MPa is about 3 500 h; at 595 °C under the same stress about 220 h.
- The operator log shows the tube-side flow control valve was found 4 % open when the heater was lit, and the bypass valve was found 100 % open. A procedure revision issued five months earlier had swapped the two valve line-items in the lighting checklist.
- Wall thickness measured at the rupture: 1.9 mm against 3.4 mm nominal; adjacent tubes 3.2-3.3 mm with visible necking.
- The client asserts in its brief: "API 560 requires fired heater tubes to be inspected for creep every year, so the owner is per se negligent." API 560 governs design and fabrication of fired heaters; API 570/573 govern in-service inspection and the creep-range damage mechanism; API 560 does not impose an annual creep inspection interval.

Tasks. (1) Build the causal chain from the facts: distinguish the mechanism of tube failure from the initiating operational condition from the contributing management-system failures, and say explicitly which link in the chain would be weakest if you had to defend your conclusion in cross-examination. (2) Decide whether the over-temperature was the cause of the rupture or a symptom, and justify using the thickness and creep numbers. (3) Show the arithmetic: estimate the accumulated creep damage fraction using linear (Robinson) damage summation for the two exchangers, stating each assumption you make where the facts are missing, and identify what additional measurement would most change your estimate. (4) Rule on the client's API 560 assertion and explain, as a matter of reasoning method rather than citation, how you decide which standard governs a given allegation. (5) List what you would need but do not have. Show your working throughout, including hypotheses you considered and rejected and why.
