A 34-year-old woman presents with 3 months of muscle cramps and salt craving. BP 104/66 mmHg (no hypertension). Labs: Na 133, K 2.7, Cl 94, HCO3 34 mmol/L, creatinine 0.8, Mg 0.58 mmol/L (low), calcium normal. Urine: K 78 mmol/L, Na 96 mmol/L, Cl 104 mmol/L. Fractional excretion of calcium is LOW (FeCa 0.8%). She takes no diuretics; urine diuretic screen negative. Renin and aldosterone are both elevated.
Work through the differential diagnosis as a nephrologist would: enumerate every cause of hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis with normal-to-low blood pressure, then eliminate each one by explicit reference to a specific number or finding above, and state the single most likely diagnosis plus the confirmatory test. Explain the mechanistic reason behind each elimination step, including why the urinary chloride and the fractional calcium excretion discriminate the way they do, and why Mg is low. Reason out loud; show false leads you consider and reject.
