Hypothetical. A Korean court entered judgment on 3 March 2027 holding that a Korean licensee, HanPlay, owns the copyright in a mobile game UI as Korean law requires, and dismissing the Dutch licensor's claim. The Dutch licensor now sues in England, claiming copyright in the same UI for England and for France, and HanPlay pleads the Korean judgment as an issue estoppel on ownership. Assume the UK is bound by neither Brussels I nor the Lugano Convention, and that no treaty on recognition of foreign judgments applies.
Using ONLY the reasoning method of the Court of Appeal in [2026] EWCA Civ 1224 (characterisation that is internationalist and non-circular; separation of the nature of a rule from its effect; the scheme argument about which instrument houses recognition and enforcement; and the absurdity check), decide by which law the plea must be determined for (a) the English copyright claim and (b) the French copyright claim. Then state, as a numbered set of decision rules, the general algorithm a commercial lawyer should apply to any plea of estoppel founded on a third-State judgment, and identify the two places where your answer would flip if a harmonised instrument on recognition of judgments came into force.
Show your working: where each step comes from, what you considered and rejected, and where the analogy to the Aviator judgment breaks down rather than holds.
